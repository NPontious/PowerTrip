What did that drive actually cost?

PowerTrip reads your car's own sensors and tells you what each drive cost, in gasoline and electricity, to the cent. Every number is measured by the car or worked out from your receipts. Nothing is modeled.

What it does

Every drive, accounted for

No "saved vs. a gas car", no guessed air drag, no expected MPGe. Just what the car did and what it cost you.

The cost of every drive

Split between electricity and gasoline, priced from what you actually paid, oldest fuel first. A free charge costs exactly $0, and drive totals add up to the cent.

Fill-ups and charges, found for you

When the tank rises while you're parked, PowerTrip notices and asks for the total from the receipt. Charging at home is measured and priced on its own, so you only type in what the car can't know.

Where the energy went, minute by minute

Battery power and fuel flow across the whole drive, lined up with speed and hills. See when the engine ran, when you were stopped, and when regen put energy back.

Compared with your own drives

Every drive is held up against your typical for the same car: efficiency in each speed band, cost per mile, and fuel-free share. Not a sticker number, and never a score.

Spending over time

What you spent on energy, cost per mile week by week, a calendar of what each day cost, and every fill-up and charge in one list.

Also in PowerTrip

Trouble codes in plain English

Which system is complaining, when it was read, during which drive, and whether the car is safe to drive. No clear button that hides the problem.

All your cars in one garage

Each car keeps its own history and typical, and you can compare cost per mile across all of them.

Your data, your way

Merge a drive that got split by a quick stop, and export a drive or your whole history as CSV.

Why PowerTrip

Why not just use what's out there?

Each kind of car app answers a different question. None of them can tell you what one drive cost.

Fuel log apps

Fuelly, GasBuddy

You type in the odometer at every fill-up and get an average per tank. They can't price a single drive, and can't split a plug-in hybrid's electricity from its gas.

PowerTrip prices each drive from the car's own readings.

OBD dashboard apps

Torque Pro, OBD Fusion

Live gauges for a phone on the windshield while you drive. Lots of numbers in the moment, nothing about what the drive cost afterwards.

PowerTrip is for after the drive, with no phone in the car.

Carmaker apps

The app that came with your car

Often a monthly subscription, with your driving history kept on the carmaker's servers, and estimated figures rather than what you paid.

PowerTrip runs on your server and uses your receipts.

Your car

Will it work with my car?

Almost any US car from 2008 on reports speed, distance and fuel level, and that's enough for a cost per drive. How much detail you get depends on what else your car shares.

  • Measured

    The car reports battery power and fuel flow second by second.

    You get everything: cost, efficiency, and where every bit of energy went.

  • Calculated

    Fuel is worked out from the engine's airflow, or battery energy from the change in charge level.

    You get cost and efficiency per drive, labeled as calculated.

  • From fill-ups

    The car only reports its fuel or charge level.

    You get cost per drive from your fill-ups and charges, labeled as such, with no breakdown.

Every drive says which of these it got. Anything the data can't support is left out rather than guessed. The 2017 Ford Fusion Energi is the reference car, with its battery signals verified on the real car.

Electric, plug-in, hybrid or gas

The same words mean the same thing on every car. A car only adds a line when it has a second energy source.

Electric

Volvo EX30

Efficiency in mi/kWh. Every mile is a fuel-free mile.

Plug-in hybrid

Ford Fusion Energi

mi/kWh and mpg side by side, each priced from its own charges and fill-ups.

Hybrid

Toyota RAV4 Hybrid

mpg, plus the miles it moved with no fuel burning and the energy regen put back per mile.

Gas

Volvo XC90 B6

mpg. Coasting with the fuel cut off still counts as fuel-free.

How it works

From the car to your own server

Two pieces do the work: an adapter in the car and a server you run at home. We recommend the WiCAN Pro adapter.

  1. The adapter logs every drive

    A WiCAN Pro in the OBD-II port records the car's signals while you drive, on its own. It needs no phone and no cell plan, and sleeps when the car is off.

  2. It syncs when you get home

    Parked on your Wi-Fi, it uploads the drive to your server in a few seconds.

  3. Your server does the math

    The PowerTrip server runs in Docker on hardware you own: a NAS, a Raspberry Pi or a home server. It prices each drive and keeps the whole history.

Your data stays on your server. There's no PowerTrip cloud between your car and you, and nobody else sees where you drive.
